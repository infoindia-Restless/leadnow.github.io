Theory of Change

From the inside out. From the ground up.

When young people, especially those with fewer opportunities, are supported, trusted and resourced, they become a sustained force for positive change in their communities.

Our conviction

Nurture the spark. Walk alongside it.

Change often begins quietly, in the moment a young person discovers that their voice matters. From that spark it moves outward, into families, communities and institutions.

Individual transformation and wider change are deeply linked. Our work is to nurture that first spark and stay alongside young people as it grows into collective action and, over time, into systems that listen to them.

The change pathway

Change moves outward, ring by ring.

It starts with one young person at the centre and widens through three stages toward our vision. Select a ring to see how we work at each stage.

  1. Starting point

    The spark

    A young person discovers that their voice matters. Everything else grows from this moment.

  2. Stage 1

    Individual leadership

    Young people develop confidence, skills and agency through experience and the belief that their leadership is needed.

    • Leadership training and experiential learning
    • Mentorship and coaching
    • Life skills, civic engagement and systems thinking
    • Exposure visits and peer learning spaces
  3. Stage 2

    Community impact

    Young leaders unite around shared challenges, mobilise peers and co-create solutions for their streets, schools and neighbourhoods.

    • Youth-led community action projects
    • Youth Hacks to solve local challenges
    • Community campaigns and collective action
    • Youth collectives and platforms
  4. Stage 3

    Lasting change

    Youth leadership becomes a recognised part of institutions and governance, broadening who leads, who is heard and who contributes.

    • Strengthening youth-led organisations
    • Cross-sector partnerships and coalitions
    • Policy dialogue and constructive engagement
    • Flexible funding for youth priorities
  5. Vision

    Thriving communities led by young people

    Inclusive and resilient communities where young people lead collective action and influence systems for lasting change.

Levels of change

What changes, and where.

We track change at four levels, from a single young person to the policies that shape their lives.

Individual

Agency deepens

Confidence grows. A young person stops seeing themselves as a product of their circumstances and starts reshaping them.

Community

Led from within

Young leaders organise real people around real problems. Communities become more cohesive and capable, led from within rather than managed from outside.

Institutional

Youth-led work is valued

Youth-led organisations grow stronger. Funders and partners recognise the value of youth-led work, and it becomes part of how development is done.

Lasting

Policies reflect reality

Governance becomes more responsive. Spaces that once felt distant begin to include young people's perspectives, because they helped shape them.

Long-term vision

Thriving, inclusive and resilient communities shaped by young people who lead collective action and influence systems for lasting social change.
