How we hold ourselves to account.

Young people can only lead when they are safe and when the organisations around them can be trusted. These policies set out how we protect people, money and integrity.

Raise a concern

If someone is at risk, act now.

If anyone is in immediate danger, call 112 first. Then report the concern through one of these routes. You do not need proof, and you will not face retaliation for raising a concern in good faith.

Our organisational policies

Each policy applies to staff, Board members, young leaders, volunteers, interns and consultants, and is written around our five values. They are built on Indian law, including the POCSO Act, the POSH Act, the DPDP Act 2023 and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023.

Safeguarding Policy

How everyone connected to Lead Now Foundation prevents harm to children, young people and vulnerable adults, recognises it, and acts when something goes wrong. Young people are treated as partners in keeping each other safe.

What it covers
  • Four duties: recognise, respond, record, report
  • Prohibited behaviours and code of conduct
  • Step-by-step reporting and case response
  • Safe recruitment and pre-employment checks
  • Types and signs of abuse, including online abuse
  • Safe programme design and do no harm
  • Consent, images and personal data of under-18s
  • Partner due diligence checklist

Fraud, Bribery and Conflict of Interest Policy

Our zero-tolerance position on fraud, bribery and corruption, and how we declare and manage conflicts of interest. It explains how to report a concern, how we protect people who report, and how we respond to incidents.

What it covers
  • Declaring and reviewing conflicts of interest
  • Examples of fraud, including external scams
  • Bribery, facilitation payments and the law
  • Gifts and hospitality thresholds
  • Roles of the Board, CEO and staff
  • Eight-step incident response
  • Declaration of Interests form
  • Gifts and Hospitality Register

Prevention of Sexual Harassment Notice

Our zero-tolerance notice under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013. It sets out who is protected, what counts as sexual harassment, and how to complain to the Internal Complaints Committee.

What it covers
  • Who is protected, including volunteers and visitors
  • What counts as sexual harassment, online and offline
  • How and when to complain to the ICC
  • Confidentiality and protection from retaliation
  • Emergency and women helpline numbers

Finance Policy and Procedures
